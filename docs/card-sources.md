# Card sources

*Last updated: 17 October 2026.*

Every factual claim on the routing prototype's end-point cards, with where
it comes from. A claim is anything the card states as fact: what a tool
supports, what a test assumes, which direction a bias goes, what powerkit
runs and how it was validated.

**Status:**
- **sourced:** the claim appears in a source that was opened and read: one
  the repository records (`coverage.md` and its source tags, the validation
  log, the engine code, `conventions.md`, `decisions.md`, the build guide),
  or one listed under "Sources opened" below;
- **partly sourced:** part of the claim is supported, part isn't (the
  "What it supports" column says which part);
- **candidate, not verified:** a likely source was found but couldn't be
  opened from here (a paywall, or a site the environment can't reach). Its
  details are given for the maintainer to check;
- **contradicted:** an opened source says something different from the
  card. The card is unchanged; the maintainer decides;
- **unsourced:** nothing was found;
- **no claim:** the text describes the design only.

The table was built from `app/routing/tree.yaml`. Each tool line was
checked automatically against `coverage.md` (the package's name, and each
function the line names), and the other claims by hand. A test checks that
every card with tools, card text or card points has rows here. Where a row
cites `coverage.md`, that page's own source tags ([Sp], [SP], [p4m], [IP],
[GP] and so on) give the original source and say whether it was read
first-hand. Sources outside the repository are cited only if they were
opened and read on 16 October 2026; nothing is cited from memory.

**Counts:** partly sourced: 23, sourced: 155, no claim: 1

## Contradictions

None open. Resolved:

- **E45, card point on autocorrelation (resolved 17 October 2026).** The card said autocorrelation makes standard power calculations "too optimistic". Zhang et al. (2011), Table 1, show that holds for positive autocorrelation only: with negative autocorrelation power is higher. The maintainer changed the point to: "Autocorrelation makes standard power calculations wrong: too optimistic when it's positive, too pessimistic when it's negative." Now sourced to Zhang et al. (2011), Table 1.
- **E45, status line (resolved 17 October 2026).** "No ready-made tool" was contradicted by PITS 0.1.0 (CRAN, 30 June 2026); the card now says a recently released package addresses the known-breakpoint case.
- **E7, E8, method wording (resolved 17 October 2026).** "Exact (analytic)" was contradicted by Superpower's own description of `ANOVA_exact()`; the cards now say "Exact dataset (no repeated simulation)".
